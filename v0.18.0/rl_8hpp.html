Detailed Description

snicholls::ml reinforcement learning for language models: GRPO's group-normalised advantages, the clipped importance-ratio objective with the k3 KL penalty and an entropy bonus, the normalisations of GRPO, DAPO and Dr. GRPO, and PPO's GAE and value loss.

Overview. Reinforcement learning from rewards samples responses from the policy, scores them, and raises the probability of the better ones (TRAINING.md T13). A rollout is plain data, rollout_view: prompts · group_size sequences padded to one length, laid out group by group (the G samples of one prompt are consecutive rows), their targets, the response mask (1 on response tokens, 0 on the prompt and padding) and one reward a sequence. The arithmetic, in the order of a step:

  1. Advantages. GRPO (Shao et al. 2024, DeepSeekMath, §4.1.2; DeepSeek-R1) uses no value model: each sample's advantage is its reward normalised within its group, Â_i = (r_i - mean(r)) / std(r), the same for every token of the response (group_advantages with advantage_scale::group_std, then expand_rows). Dr. GRPO (Liu et al. 2025, "Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training", appendix A eq. 6) drops the division by the std, which weights questions of low reward variance more (advantage_scale::none). PPO estimates per-token advantages with a value model and GAE (gae_rows).
  2. Ratios. r_t = π_θ(o_t) / π_old(o_t) = exp(logp_t - old_logp_t), from the log-probabilities of the policy being trained and of the policy that sampled (ml/logprobs.hpp); 1 on the first step after sampling.
  3. The objective of a token, to be minimised (policy_token_loss): -min(r_t Â_t, clip(r_t, 1 - ε_low, 1 + ε_high) Â_t) + β D_KL,t - c_H H_t, the clipped surrogate of PPO (Schulman et al. 2017) as GRPO's eq. 3 writes it, DAPO's asymmetric clip ("clip-higher", ε_low = 0.2, ε_high = 0.28; Yu et al. 2025, eq. 10), the KL to the reference by the k3 estimator π_ref/π_θ - log(π_ref/π_θ) - 1 (GRPO's eq. 4; GRPO's β = 0.04, DAPO's 0), and an entropy bonus c_H H_t (the entropy of the token's distribution, from token_logprobs_rows).

  4. Aggregation over a batch, the choice the variants disagree on (token_aggregation):

    • sequence_mean: GRPO's (1/G) Σ_i (1/|o_i|) Σ_t, each response's mean, then the mean over responses. Every response weighs the same, so a token of a long response weighs less: Dr. GRPO shows this favours short correct and long wrong answers.
    • token_mean: DAPO's 1 / Σ_i |o_i| · Σ_i Σ_t (eq. 12), every token of the batch the same weight.
    • constant: Dr. GRPO's Σ_t ... / MAX_TOKENS per response, then the mean over responses: a constant (max_tokens, by default the padded length) in place of each length, so neither bias appears.

    policy_loss_rows computes the aggregated loss and the gradient in every token's log-probability (and in its entropy), ready for token_logprobs_rows_backward.

  5. PPO's value loss (value_loss_rows): ½ max((V - R)², (clip(V, V_old ± ε_v) - R)²) over response tokens, with the returns R = Â + V_old from GAE.
// A rollout as plain data, then one GRPO step: advantages within each group, the clipped objective with the
// k3 KL to the reference, and the gradient in each token's log-probability, then in the logits
const ml::rollout_view<double> rollout{prompts, group, 1, targets, mask, rewards};
std::vector<double> adv(rows), adv_tok(rows), logps(rows), ref_logps(rows), dlogps(rows);
ml::group_advantages<double>(backend, rollout.rewards, rollout.group_size, adv);
ml::expand_rows<double>(backend, adv, adv_tok);
ml::token_logprobs_rows<double>(backend, logits, rollout.targets, logps);
ml::token_logprobs_rows<double>(backend, ref_logits, rollout.targets, ref_logps);
const auto& old_logps = logps; // one step per rollout: the sampling policy is the policy, ratios 1
ml::policy_loss_metrics<double> metrics;
ml::policy_loss_rows<double>(backend, rollout.response_mask, logps, old_logps, ref_logps, adv_tok, {}, dlogps, {},
rollout.rows(), config, metrics);
ml::token_logprobs_rows_backward<double>(backend, logits, rollout.targets, dlogps); // logits: their gradient

Relationships. ml/logprobs.hpp (log-probabilities, entropies and their backward), ml/reward_model.hpp (the value head), ml/preference.hpp (the offline alternative). Sampling the rollouts is ml/sampling.hpp's; the rollout loop, the reward functions and the weights' synchronisation belong to the programs above core. Group: snicholls-core.

Implementations. One each, on the host; the backend-first functions are the seam for a device (TRAINING.md T9).

Guarantees. constexpr, no allocation, noexcept for the standard floating-point types; linear in the tokens. Every gradient is written, not added. policy_loss_metrics merges across workers.

Errors. Preconditions, asserted: sizes agree (rows · length per-token spans, rows per-sequence spans); the rows divide into groups of group_size. A group whose rewards are all equal has advantage 0 in every sample (no signal: DAPO's dynamic sampling drops such groups before the step; core leaves that to the caller). A response of no tokens contributes nothing.

Definitions. Checked against the papers: GRPO's objective, the k3 estimator and the outcome advantage (arXiv 2402.03300 eqs. 3-4, §4.1.2); DAPO's token-level loss, clip-higher and no KL (2503.14476 eqs. 8-12, §2.3, §4.1); Dr. GRPO's removals and MAX_TOKENS (2503.20783 §3.2, appendix A). Recalled, not re-read: the std of group_std is the sample std (n - 1) plus ε = 1e-4, as TRL's GRPO trainer computes it (the paper does not say); PPO's clipped surrogate, GAE (Schulman et al. 2016) and the clipped value loss of OpenAI's PPO2 baseline; masked_whiten's sample variance and ε = 1e-8, as TRL's.

Gradient at the clip. Where the unclipped and clipped terms are equal (inside the range, or exactly on its edge) the derivative is the unclipped one, -r_t Â_t; strictly outside, on the side the clip binds, it is 0. The same convention for the value loss.

Performance. policy_loss_rows (GRPO's settings, with the metrics) against the same loop by hand, after a bit-for-bit check (benchmarks/ml/rl_benchmark.cpp), 64 sequences of 1024 tokens: 1.9 ms against 2.1 ms, level (Xeon W-3245, -O2, Apple clang 17, 2026-11-19, one thread, CPU time, load average about 100: a record, not a quiet-machine figure). Negligible next to the passes that produce its inputs.

Tests and demos. tests/ml/rl_test.cpp, demos/ml/rl_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <cassert>, <cstddef>, <cstdint>, <span>, <type_traits>, snicholls/core/math/constexpr_math.hpp.

Known issues. Groups are consecutive rows of one size; a rollout with groups of different sizes is split by the caller. Sequence-level importance ratios (GSPO) are not here.

Status. Experimental, new in 0.16.0.
