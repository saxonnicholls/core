Detailed Description

The Snell envelope: the value of stopping optimally on a finite Markov chain, its stopping region, and its Doob-Meyer compensator.

Overview. Holding a right to stop (exercise an option, close a trade) and collect g(t, s) in state s at time t, the best you can do is the Snell envelope: the smallest supermartingale above the payoff. On a finite chain it is backward induction, U_T = g(T, .) and U_t = max(g(t, .), discount * E[U_{t+1} | .]); stop the first time U_t = g(t, s_t).

// A spread in five states, -2 .. 2, drifting back towards 0. Closing in state s pays s; ten days to go.
constexpr std::array<std::array<double, 5>, 5> drift_to_zero{{{0.5, 0.5, 0, 0, 0},
{0.1, 0.4, 0.5, 0, 0},
{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.25, 0},
{0, 0, 0.5, 0.4, 0.1},
{0, 0, 0, 0.5, 0.5}}};
constexpr auto close_now = [](std::size_t, std::size_t s) { return static_cast<double>(s) - 2.0; };
constexpr auto plan = st::snell_envelope<5, 10>(drift_to_zero, close_now);
// Computed by the compiler: at the top, take the profit; anywhere below, waiting is worth more.
static_assert(plan.stops(0, 4) && !plan.stops(0, 2));

The envelope's Doob-Meyer decomposition is U = M - A with A increasing; its increment U_t - discount * E[U_{t+1}] is zero exactly where continuing is optimal, and positive where waiting loses value. compensator_increment is that increment. stops is "the payoff is at least what continuing is worth", so ties stop and stopping_time is the smallest optimal stopping time; the first time the compensator rises is the largest.

Two forms: snell_envelope<States, Horizon> takes a std::array transition matrix and is constexpr throughout, so a stopping table for a small chain is computed by the compiler; the markov_chain form takes any size at run time.

Relationships. stochastic/martingale.hpp; math/constexpr_math.hpp for payoffs at compile time. Continuous models (LSM, HJB solvers) belong in the consuming library. Group: snicholls-core.

Guarantees. O(Horizon x States^2) arithmetic; the fixed form allocates nothing. value(t, s) is at least g(t, s) and is a supermartingale along the chain.

Errors. None.

Performance. Not benchmarked.

Tests and demos. tests/stochastic/snell_test.cpp (Hull's American put), demos/stochastic/snell_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <array>, <cstddef>, <functional>, <limits>, <span>, <vector>, stochastic/martingale.hpp.

Known issues. Discrete time and finite states: discretising a diffusion is the caller's.

Status. New in 0.5.0, experimental.
