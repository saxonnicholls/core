Detailed Description

snicholls::signal FIR filter design by the windowed sinc (scipy.signal.firwin): low-pass, high-pass, band-pass and band-stop, constexpr so a filter can be a compile-time table, with direct filtering and the frequency response.

Overview. fir_design(h, band, f1, f2, window) is scipy.signal.firwin(numtaps, cutoff, window, pass_zero, scale=True) with frequencies as fractions of the Nyquist frequency (scipy's default fs = 2): the ideal response's impulse response, sum over pass bands (right sinc(right m) - left sinc(left m)) with m = i - (taps - 1) / 2, multiplied by a symmetric window (Hamming by default, as scipy's), then scaled to unit gain at the centre of the first pass band (0 for a low-pass, Nyquist for a high-pass). A high-pass or band-stop filter passes Nyquist, so it needs an odd number of taps.

make_fir<T, N>(band, f1, f2, window) returns the taps as a std::array: constexpr auto h = make_fir<float, 63>(fir_band::lowpass, 0.25); is computed by the compiler. fir_filter applies taps directly (scipy.signal.lfilter(h, 1, x) from rest); fir_response evaluates H(e^(i omega)).

// 63 taps, cut-off at a quarter of Nyquist, Hamming window: designed by the compiler, scipy's firwin(63, 0.25).
constexpr auto lowpass = s::make_fir<float, 63>(s::fir_band::lowpass, 0.25f);
// A low tone (0.05 of the sample rate) plus a high one (0.4): the filter keeps the first.
std::vector<float> x(400), y(400);
for (std::size_t i = 0; i < x.size(); ++i)
x[i] = static_cast<float>(m::sin(2 * m::pi * 0.05 * static_cast<double>(i)) + m::sin(2 * m::pi * 0.4 * static_cast<double>(i)));
s::fir_filter<float>(lowpass, x, y);
// The other bands, at run time into a span, with a Kaiser window.
std::vector<double> band(101), stop(101), high(101);
s::fir_design(std::span<double>(band), s::fir_band::bandpass, 0.2, 0.4, s::kaiser{8.0});
s::fir_design(std::span<double>(stop), s::fir_band::bandstop, 0.2, 0.4, s::kaiser{8.0});
s::fir_design(std::span<double>(high), s::fir_band::highpass, 0.5);

Relationships. signal/window.hpp (the windows), signal/convolve.hpp (FFT filtering for long filters), signal/resample.hpp (its anti-aliasing filter is a fir_design low-pass). Group: snicholls-core.

Implementations. The windowed sinc only; least-squares and Parks-McClellan designs are not provided.

Guarantees. constexpr, no allocation. Taps are symmetric (linear phase). Computed in compute_t<T>. Complexity: O(taps) to design, O(taps) per output sample to filter.

Errors. Preconditions, asserted: 0 < f1 < 1 (and f1 < f2 < 1 for the band forms); an odd number of taps for highpass and bandstop, as scipy requires.

Performance. Not benchmarked: design runs once; fir_filter is the direct convolution (signal/convolve.hpp's benchmark).

Tests and demos. tests/signal/fir_test.cpp (scipy 1.13.1's taps, the responses at sample frequencies, compile-time design), demos/signal/fir_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <array>, <cassert>, <cstddef>, <span>, <type_traits>, signal/complex.hpp, signal/window.hpp.

Known issues. scipy also accepts several bands and a sample rate; here there is at most one band and the frequencies are normalised.

Status. Experimental, new in 0.17.0.
