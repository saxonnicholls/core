Detailed Description

snicholls::optim: Euclidean projections onto the cones and sets of conic optimisation, their membership tests and dual cones, and Nesterov-Todd scaling.

Overview. A first-order conic solver (OSQP's ADMM, SCS's ADMM on the homogeneous embedding, PDLP) spends its iterations projecting onto a product of simple cones, and an interior-point solver (Clarabel's) scales by the Nesterov-Todd point of each. These are those primitives, in place on a std::span:

Set Projection Membership Dual
zero {0} / free Rⁿ project_zero, project_free in_zero_cone, in_free_cone each other
non-negative orthant project_nonnegative in_nonnegative_cone self
box [l, u] (infinite bounds allowed) project_box in_box not a cone
second-order cone {(t, x) : ‖x‖ ≤ t} project_second_order in_second_order_cone self
rotated {(u, v, w) : 2uv ≥ ‖w‖², u, v ≥ 0} project_rotated_second_order in_rotated_second_order_cone self
PSD, scaled svec project_psd, project_psd_fixed<N> in_psd_cone, in_psd_cone_fixed<N> self
exponential cl{(x, y, z) : y > 0, y e^{x/y} ≤ z} project_exponential in_exponential_cone project_dual_exponential

| power {(x, y, z) : x^α y^{1-α} ≥ |z|, x, y ≥ 0} | project_power | in_power_cone | project_dual_power | | simplex {x ≥ 0, Σx = r} | project_simplex | in_simplex | not a cone | | ℓ1 ball, ℓ∞ ball, capped simplex | project_l1_ball, project_linf_ball, project_capped_simplex | in_l1_ball, ... | not cones |

A product of cones laid out one after another is described by an array of cone_kind (type, dimension, parameter) and projected by project(cones, x, workspace), onto its dual by project_dual, and tested by in_cone. nt_scaling_nonnegative and nt_scaling_second_order compute the Nesterov-Todd scaling of a pair of interior points, nt_apply_* apply W or W⁻¹ in place.

Conventions.

  • PSD matrices are in scaled svec form, as SCS and Clarabel store them: the lower triangle column by column, (0,0), (1,0), ..., (n-1,0), (1,1), (2,1), ..., each off-diagonal element times √2, so that svec(A) · svec(B) = tr(AB) and the projection in svec space is the projection in the Frobenius norm. A matrix (for svec / smat) is n × n, row-major. A cone_kind of PSD has dimension = n(n+1)/2.
  • The second-order cone's first element is t; the rotated cone's first two are u and v (MOSEK's convention, 2uv ≥ ‖w‖²).
  • Nesterov-Todd: for s and z in the interior of a self-dual cone, W is the symmetric positive definite matrix with W z = W⁻¹ s (the scaled variable λ); equivalently W² z = s. For the orthant W = diag(w), wᵢ = √(sᵢ / zᵢ), λᵢ = √(sᵢ zᵢ). For the second-order cone, with J = diag(1, -1, ..., -1), ‖s‖_J = √(s₀² - ‖s₁‖²), s̄ = s / ‖s‖_J, z̄ = z / ‖z‖_J, γ = √((1 + s̄ᵀz̄) / 2), the normalised scaling point is w̄ = (s̄ + J z̄) / (2γ) (so ‖w̄‖_J = 1), η = √(‖s‖_J / ‖z‖_J) and W = η [[w̄₀, w̄₁ᵀ], [w̄₁, I + w̄₁w̄₁ᵀ / (1 + w̄₀)]], W⁻¹ = η⁻¹ [[w̄₀, -w̄₁ᵀ], [-w̄₁, I + w̄₁w̄₁ᵀ / (1 + w̄₀)]], W² = η² (2w̄w̄ᵀ - J) (Vandenberghe's CVXOPT notes, ECOS and Clarabel use the same).
// SCS's cone layout: equality rows, then inequalities, then a second-order, a 2 x 2 PSD (svec), an exponential and a
// power cone, one after another in one vector.
constexpr std::array<op::cone_kind, 6> layout{{{op::cone::zero, 1, 0},
{op::cone::nonnegative, 2, 0},
{op::cone::second_order, 3, 0},
{op::cone::psd, 3, 0},
{op::cone::exponential, 3, 0},
{op::cone::power, 3, 0.3}}};
// The projection step of the iteration, and the check a solver makes of its output
bool project_step(std::span<double> x, std::span<double> polar)
{
std::array<double, op::psd_workspace_size(2)> ws{}; // the largest PSD block's workspace
const std::span<const op::cone_kind> k(layout);
std::array<double, 15> minus{};
for (std::size_t i = 0; i < x.size(); ++i)
minus[i] = -x[i];
// Moreau: x = P_K(x) - P_K*(-x), and the two parts are orthogonal
if (!op::project(k, x, std::span<double>(ws)) || !op::project_dual(k, std::span<double>(minus), std::span<double>(ws)))
return false;
for (std::size_t i = 0; i < x.size(); ++i)
polar[i] = -minus[i];
return op::in_cone(k, std::span<const double>(x), 1e-12, std::span<double>(ws));
}

Relationships. optim/prox.hpp (proximal operators; the ℓ∞ prox uses the ℓ1-ball projection here), linalg/fixed.hpp (symmetric_eigen for project_psd_fixed), linalg/reference.hpp (the dense eigen decomposition for run-time n), math/constexpr_math.hpp. Group: snicholls-core.

Implementations.

  • Second-order cone: the closed form. Rotated: the orthogonal map (u, v) ↦ ((u + v)/√2, (u − v)/√2) to the ordinary cone and back.
  • PSD: eigen decomposition, then the sum over whichever of the positive or the negative eigenvalues is the shorter (X₊ = Σ_{λ>0} λ v vᵀ = X − Σ_{λ<0} λ v vᵀ). A matrix already PSD is returned unchanged.
  • Exponential cone: Friberg (2021), "Projection onto the exponential cone: a univariate root-finding problem". After the analytic cases (in the cone, in the polar cone, x ≤ 0 and y ≤ 0), the projection is s (ρ, 1, e^ρ) where ρ is the root of h(ρ) = ((ρ−1)x₀ + y₀) e^ρ − (x₀ − ρ y₀) e^{−ρ} − (ρ² − ρ + 1) z₀ in the interval where both (ρ−1)x₀ + y₀ and x₀ − ρ y₀ are positive (both are linear in ρ, so the interval is explicit; an unbounded end is bracketed by doubling). Any root there gives a Moreau decomposition, so it is the projection. h is evaluated scaled by e^{−|ρ|}, so nothing overflows, and solved by Newton safeguarded by bisection. The point is the projection onto the ray at the root, compared with Friberg's heuristic (min(x₀, 0), 0, max(z₀, 0)), and the nearer kept. The input is scaled by its largest magnitude first (the projection is positively homogeneous), so 1e±300 work as 1 does.
  • Power cone: Hien (2015), "Differential properties of Euclidean projection onto power cone": the projection is (x(r), y(r), sign(z₀) r) with x(r) = (x₀ + √(x₀² + 4α r(|z₀| − r)))/2, y(r) likewise with 1 − α, and r ∈ (0, |z₀|) the root of x(r)^α y(r)^{1−α} = r, by Newton safeguarded by bisection. x and y depend on r through r(|z₀| − r), so the root is sought as r or as |z₀| − r, whichever is below |z₀|/2: a projection with x or y near 0 is then resolved to the type's precision, not to one ulp of |z₀|.
  • Simplex and ℓ1 ball: Condat (2016), "Fast projection onto the simplex and the ℓ1 ball", expected O(n), with a caller workspace of n; without a workspace, Michelot's (1986) method, O(n) a sweep and a few sweeps.
  • Capped simplex: bisection on the threshold to adjacent floats, then the exact threshold of the final active set.

Guarantees. Every function is constexpr and noexcept for the standard floating-point types (exactly when T's arithmetic is). No allocation: the PSD projection takes a workspace of psd_workspace_size(n) elements, the simplex projection one of n. Projections are idempotent up to rounding. A point already in a cone, the box or the ℓ∞ ball is returned unchanged, bit for bit; one already in a simplex or a capped simplex to rounding. Complexity: O(n) for all but the PSD cone (O(n³) per Jacobi sweep) and the capped simplex (O(n) per bisection step, at most 200).

Errors. optim::error::dimension_mismatch for spans of different lengths, an svec length that is not triangular, an exponential or power cone of dimension other than 3, a rotated cone of dimension below 2; workspace_too_small; infeasible for a negative radius or an empty capped simplex; not_interior from the Nesterov-Todd scalings; did_not_converge from the eigen decomposition. Nothing throws.

Performance. Not benchmarked.

Tests and demos. tests/optim/cones_test.cpp, demos/optim/cones_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <array>, <cstddef>, <cstdint>, <limits>, <span>, <type_traits>, <utility>, linalg/fixed.hpp, linalg/reference.hpp (the whole reference backend, for the run-time PSD projection), math/constexpr_math.hpp, result.hpp.

Known issues. Membership tests with a tolerance are by distance for the exponential and power cones (the distance to the projection is at most tol) and by the defining inequality otherwise. The exponential and power projections are accurate to a few units in the last place of the input's largest magnitude, not of each element: an element 1e-20 times the largest is known only absolutely. The PSD projection's accuracy is the Jacobi decomposition's, about n ε ‖X‖. The power cone needs 0 < α < 1.

Status. Experimental.
