Detailed Description

snicholls::geometry::lla_to_ecef, ecef_to_lla and local_frame: WGS 84 geodetic, Earth-centred and local East-North-Up coordinates.

Overview. A GNSS fix is latitude, longitude and height above the ellipsoid; trackers work in metres on a local tangent plane. These convert between the three frames on an ellipsoid (WGS 84 by default):

  • lla_to_ecef(lla) and ecef_to_lla(ecef): geodetic (latitude, longitude, height), radians and metres, to and from Earth-centred, Earth-fixed (x, y, z) metres.
  • enu_rotation(lat, lon): the rotation from ECEF into East-North-Up at a point.
  • local_frame<T>: an ENU frame anchored at an origin, with its rotation and the origin's ECEF position computed once at construction (constexpr), then to_enu(lla), to_lla(enu), ecef_to_enu, enu_to_ecef, and to_ned / from_ned for North-East-Down.
// The session's origin, a beach at 33.89 S, 151.27 E, 2 m above the ellipsoid; built once
const geo::local_frame<double> frame(v3{{-33.8915 * deg, 151.2767 * deg, 2.0}});
// A swimmer's GNSS fix, a little to the north-east
const v3 fix{{-33.8906 * deg, 151.2781 * deg, 2.0}};
const auto enu = frame.to_enu(fix); // metres east, north, up
const auto ned = geo::local_frame<double>::to_ned(enu); // north, east, down for the flight controller
// And back: a point 50 m out to sea (east), on the tangent plane
const auto buoy = frame.to_lla(v3{{50.0, 0.0, 0.0}});

Relationships. linalg/fixed.hpp, math/constexpr_math.hpp, geometry/angle.hpp (radians). The world frame of multi-sensor fusion, anchored at a session origin. Group: snicholls-core.

Implementations. lla_to_ecef is the closed form. ecef_to_lla iterates the latitude φ ← atan2(z + e² N(φ) sin φ, p) from atan2(z, p (1 - e²)) until it stops changing, at most 64 times. The error shrinks by about e² a / r each step at distance r from the centre: three or four steps near the surface, more deep inside the Earth, so the result is exact to rounding for any point from about 100 km from the centre to geostationary orbit (within e² a, 43 km, of the centre a point has more than one geodetic latitude). The height is p cos φ + z sin φ - a √(1 - e² sin² φ), which stays exact at the poles, where p / cos φ would not.

Guarantees. constexpr, noexcept for the standard floating-point types, no allocation, no recursion. Round trips agree to well under a millimetre in double.

Errors. None. At the Earth's centre the latitude is 0 and the height is -b.

Performance. Not benchmarked: a handful of trigonometric calls each.

Tests and demos. tests/geometry/geodetic_test.cpp, demos/geometry/geodetic_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. linalg/fixed.hpp, math/constexpr_math.hpp.

Known issues. Heights are above the ellipsoid, not mean sea level: a geoid model is outside core.

Status. Experimental, new in 0.19.0.
