Detailed Description

Vanilla options priced by the compiler: Black-Scholes-Merton and its greeks in closed form, a CRR tree for American exercise.

Overview. A vanilla_option is a call or a put on an underlying paying a continuous yield q (a dividend yield, a foreign rate, a futures' r): spot, strike, years to expiry, rate, yield and volatility, all in one floating type. black_scholes prices the European option in closed form; black_scholes_greeks gives delta, gamma, vega, vanna, volga, theta and rho as a finance::greeks (so a book's greeks add, and explain takes them). binomial<Steps> prices it on a CRR tree solved by stochastic::lattice_envelope, European or American, with delta and gamma off the tree and the early-exercise boundary as a spot price per step. Every function is constexpr in the option's floating type through snicholls::math, so a price, a greek or an exercise boundary is a static_assert.

// A three-month call on an index yielding 2%: S 100, K 105, r 4%, vol 25%. Designated members, any floating type.
constexpr finance::vanilla_option<double> call{.kind = finance::option_kind::call, .spot = 100, .strike = 105, .expiry = 0.25,
.rate = 0.04, .yield = 0.02, .vol = 0.25};
constexpr double premium = finance::black_scholes(call);
constexpr finance::greeks<> risk = finance::black_scholes_greeks(call); // delta, gamma, vega, vanna, volga, theta, rho
// The put on the same terms, and put-call parity, proved by the compiler
constexpr finance::vanilla_option<double> put{.kind = finance::option_kind::put, .spot = 100, .strike = 105, .expiry = 0.25,
.rate = 0.04, .yield = 0.02, .vol = 0.25};
static_assert(math::near(premium - finance::black_scholes(put), 100 * math::exp(-0.02 * 0.25) - 105 * math::exp(-0.04 * 0.25), 1e-12));
static_assert(risk.delta > 0 && risk.delta < 1 && risk.gamma > 0 && risk.vega > 0 && risk.theta < 0);
// The put again, American, on a 100-step CRR tree solved by its Snell envelope: the price, delta and gamma off
// the tree, and the critical spot below which to exercise at each step. O(steps) state, by the compiler.
constexpr auto american = finance::binomial<100>(put);
constexpr auto european = finance::binomial<100>(put, finance::exercise_style::european);
static_assert(american.value > european.value, "the right to exercise early is worth something");
static_assert(math::abs(european.value - finance::black_scholes(put)) < 0.02, "100 steps are within 2 cents of the closed form");
static_assert(american.boundary[99] > american.boundary[50] && american.boundary[99] < put.strike, "the boundary rises to the strike");

Use these for the quick, exact-to-the-model figure: a showcase, a test oracle, a risk check. Volatility surfaces, discrete dividends, calibration and exotic payoffs belong in the libraries above core.

Relationships. stochastic/snell.hpp (lattice_envelope), finance/attribution.hpp (greeks, explain), math/constexpr_math.hpp (normal_cdf, normal_pdf, exp, log, sqrt). Group: snicholls-core.

Guarantees. constexpr, no allocation. Black-Scholes is O(1): two logarithm-and-exponential evaluations and two normal distribution functions. binomial<Steps> is O(Steps^2) time in O(Steps) std::array state, its loops bounded by Steps. The greeks are those of the closed form, in the units of the inputs: vega per unit of volatility, rho per unit of rate, theta per year of calendar time (dV/dt, the value lost as the option ages, so -dV/d(expiry)).

Errors. None;

Precondition
spot, strike, vol and expiry are positive. Outside that the arithmetic returns infinities or NaN rather than throwing.

Performance. benchmarks/finance/option_pricing_benchmark.cpp, per option over 4,096 random options against the same arithmetic by hand: black_scholes 63 ns (hand 72 ns), black_scholes_greeks 84 ns (84 ns), binomial<64> American with its boundary 6.1 us (5.3 us) (Intel Xeon W-3245, macOS 26, Apple clang 17, Release, 2026-11-26).

Tests and demos. tests/finance/option_pricing_test.cpp (Hull's examples, put-call parity, the greeks against central differences, the tree's convergence to the closed form, American against European), demos/finance/option_pricing_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <array>, <concepts>, <cstddef>, <cstdint>, <limits>, finance/attribution.hpp, math/constexpr_math.hpp, stochastic/snell.hpp.

Known issues. A CRR tree's error oscillates with the parity of Steps and falls as 1/Steps; average two adjacent step counts for a smoother figure. With float, the closed form is good to about six figures and the greeks' cancellation-prone ones (theta, vanna) to fewer; prefer double for risk.

Status. New in 0.27.0, experimental.
