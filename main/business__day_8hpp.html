Detailed Description

Business-day adjustment rules (following, modified following, preceding, modified preceding, unadjusted) over a holiday calendar the caller supplies.

Overview. A payment or fixing date that falls on a day the market is closed moves to a business day by a rule the trade names (ISDA 2006 section 4.12):

  • following: the first business day on or after the date;
  • modified_following: following, unless that is in the next month, then preceding;
  • preceding: the last business day on or before the date;
  • modified_preceding: preceding, unless that is in the previous month, then following;
  • unadjusted: the date as it is;
  • half_month_modified_following: modified following, and also not across the 15th (QuantLib);
  • nearest: the nearer business day, following on a tie (QuantLib).

Over any calendar too, from crtp's calendar (QuantLib's Calendar, 0.27.0): is_end_of_month and end_of_month (the last business day of a month), advance_business_days, advance_months (calendar months, then the convention, with the end-of-month rule), count_business_days (QuantLib's flags), holidays_between, joint_calendar (holidays of either, or business days of either) and adjusted_calendar (a calendar with holidays added and removed, without changing the one it wraps).

adjust(day, convention, calendar) applies one to any holiday_calendar. Core holds no holidays, which are run-time data: weekend_calendar (Saturday and Sunday closed) is the reference calendar, and weekends_and_holidays{predicate} makes a calendar from the caller's own holiday test, a set or a table loaded at start-up.

const sys_days saturday = sys_days{2026y / October / 31d};
const auto following = adjust(saturday, business_day_convention::following, weekend_calendar{}); // 2 November
const auto modified = adjust(saturday, business_day_convention::modified_following, weekend_calendar{}); // 30 October
// holidays are the caller's data: here a set, loaded at start-up
const std::set<sys_days> holidays{sys_days{2026y / December / 25d}, sys_days{2026y / December / 28d}};
const weekends_and_holidays market{[&](sys_days d) { return holidays.contains(d); }};
const auto boxing_week = adjust(sys_days{2026y / December / 25d}, business_day_convention::following, market); // 29 December

Relationships. concepts/holiday_calendar.hpp, interfaces/holiday_calendar.hpp (a calendar chosen at run time), time/business_days.hpp (counting Monday-to-Friday days), finance/day_count.hpp. Group: snicholls-core.

Implementations. The seven rules; weekend_calendar; weekends_and_holidays<F>; joint_calendar<A, B>; adjusted_calendar<C>.

Guarantees. constexpr, no allocation, noexcept when the calendar's is_business_day is. O(g) calls of the calendar for a run of g closed days. A business day is returned unchanged by every rule.

Errors. business_day_errc::no_business_day when the search passes max_business_day_search closed days in a row: a calendar with no open day in a year is broken, and the search stops rather than loop forever.

Performance. A weekend-only calendar adjusts in at most three tests; not benchmarked.

Tests and demos. tests/finance/business_day_test.cpp, against fixtures from QuantLib 1.44 over its WeekendsOnly and TARGET calendars (scripts/generate_finance_fixtures.py, tests/fixtures/finance/business_day.txt); demos/finance/business_day_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <algorithm>, <chrono>, <cstdint>, <string_view>, <utility>, <vector>, concepts/holiday_calendar.hpp, result.hpp, time/business_days.hpp.

Known issues. holidays_between allocates its vector. The two QuantLib rules added in 0.27.0 are checked against hand-computed dates, not against QuantLib's fixtures.

Status. New in 0.23.0; the QuantLib rules and calendar operations in 0.27.0.
