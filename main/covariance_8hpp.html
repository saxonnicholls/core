Detailed Description

Covariance matrices from a history of returns: the sample estimate and Ledoit and Wolf's shrinkage.

Overview. With many assets and a short history the sample covariance is noisy and badly conditioned: its smallest eigenvalues are too small, so an optimiser leans on them and risk contributions swing from day to day. Ledoit and Wolf (2004) shrink it towards a multiple of the identity by the amount that minimises the expected error, estimated from the data:

// The sample estimate is noisy with 40 dates and 20 assets; Ledoit and Wolf shrink it towards the
// average variance by the amount that minimises the expected error.
const stats::covariance_matrix sample = stats::sample_covariance(returns);
const stats::covariance_matrix shrunk = stats::ledoit_wolf(returns);

The data are rows of returns, one row per date, each a random-access range of the same length (one column per asset). sample_covariance is the maximum-likelihood estimate (divided by T, about each column's mean), the one Ledoit and Wolf shrink; ledoit_wolf returns the shrunk matrix and the shrinkage, the same as scikit-learn's LedoitWolf.

Relationships. finance/attribution.hpp's risk_contributions takes the result; stats/accumulators.hpp's covariance for one pair, streaming. Group: snicholls-core.

Guarantees. The result is symmetric. The shrinkage is in [0, 1]; the shrunk matrix is positive definite whenever any column varies. O(T N^2) time, O(N^2) memory.

Errors. None: rows of different lengths are a precondition violation (asserted); no rows give an empty matrix.

Performance. Not benchmarked; two passes over the data.

Tests and demos. tests/stats/covariance_test.cpp, against scikit-learn; demos/stats/covariance_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <cassert>, <cstddef>, <ranges>, <vector>.

Known issues. The target is a scaled identity; Ledoit and Wolf's constant-correlation and single-index targets are not provided.

Status. New in 0.5.0.
