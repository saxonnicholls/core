Detailed Description

Exact arithmetic between money and finance::ratio: an amount times or divided by a ratio, as an exact ratio or as money at a stated scale with a named rounding mode; the ratio of two amounts; _ratio literals.

Overview. A fund's dealing is units = amount / NAV per unit and amount = units * NAV per unit, where the NAV per unit is a pure rational, not tied to any asset (1.234567 AUD a unit is ratio{1234567, 1000000}). These functions do that arithmetic exactly, with one rounding at the end, where the caller names it:

  • exact_value(m): an amount as a ratio of whole units (1000.00 AUD is 1000/1);
  • multiply(m, r) and divide(m, r): the exact product or quotient, as a ratio of whole units of the result;
  • multiply(m, r, out, mode) and divide(m, r, out, mode): the same, as money of the asset out, rounded once to its scale under mode; computed in 256 bits, so any amount money holds works;
  • to_money(r, out, mode): a ratio of whole units as money of out;
  • ratio_of(a, b): a / b for two amounts of any assets (net assets over units outstanding is the NAV per unit);
  • "1.234567"_ratio (finance::literals): a ratio checked by the compiler.
constexpr ratio nav_per_unit = "1.234567"_ratio; // a pure rational, no asset
const money subscription = *parse_money(aud, "1000.00");
const auto exact = divide(subscription, nav_per_unit); // 1000000000/1234567 units, exactly
const auto issued = divide(subscription, nav_per_unit, units, rounding::toward_zero); // 810.0005, one rounding
const auto value = multiply(*issued, nav_per_unit, aud, rounding::toward_zero); // 999.99 (of 999.99988)

Relationships. finance/ratio.hpp, money/money.hpp (rounding), money/rate.hpp (an asset-to-asset rate, for currency conversion; a ratio carries no assets). Group: snicholls-core.

Implementations / variants. Exact ratio results (64-bit parts) or money results (256-bit intermediate).

Guarantees. constexpr and noexcept; no allocation. A money result is the exact value rounded once under the named mode; the sign is the product's.

Errors. std::nullopt for a division by zero, an amount whose value does not fit a ratio (the ratio forms), or a result beyond 256 bits (the money forms). A malformed _ratio literal is a compile error.

Performance. Not benchmarked: a 256-bit multiply and divide (benchmarks/money/ measures those).

Tests and demos. tests/finance/money_ratio_test.cpp, demos/finance/money_ratio_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <cstddef>, <cstdint>, <limits>, <optional>, <string_view>, finance/ratio.hpp, money/money.hpp, uint256.hpp.

Known issues. The ratio forms keep 64-bit parts: an amount in wei with 18 decimals rarely fits; use the money forms, which do not have that limit.

Status. New in 0.27.0.
