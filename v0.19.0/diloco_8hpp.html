Detailed Description

snicholls::ml arithmetic of data-parallel training across machines: the fixed-order sum of the workers' parts, DiLoCo's pseudo-gradient, its bfloat16 compression with error feedback, and its outer step.

Overview. Data-parallel training runs one model on many workers, each on its own data, and merges what they learn. Two ways of merging share this header's arithmetic:

  • Every step (TRAINING.md T5): each worker's gradients are summed across workers (an all-reduce) before the optimiser steps. ordered_sum(parts, out) is that sum's reference.
  • Rarely: DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2023, "DiLoCo: Distributed Low-Communication Training of Language Models"). Every worker starts a round from the same global weights, takes H local steps with its own inner optimiser (AdamW in the paper) on its own data, and only then sends the change of its weights, the pseudo-gradient global - local (pseudo_gradient). The pseudo-gradients are averaged (ordered_mean, or ordered_sum and a division by the worker count) and an outer optimiser steps the global weights with the mean as its gradient (diloco_outer_step). The paper's H is hundreds of steps (500 in its main runs), so the network carries one exchange of the weights per H steps instead of one per step: for clusters, or machines across sites, whose network is too slow for an all-reduce every step. Its outer optimiser is SGD with Nesterov momentum 0.9 and learning rate 0.7, the defaults of diloco_config; with momentum 0, learning rate 1 and no Nesterov the outer step replaces the global weights by the workers' mean (federated averaging).

One round, as the demo runs it:

for (std::size_t r = 0; r < rounds; ++r) {
for (std::size_t k = 0; k < workers; ++k) { // on worker k, its own machine
std::array<double, dim> local = global;
for (std::size_t t = 0; t < h; ++t)
sgd_step(local, streams[k]);
ml::pseudo_gradient<double>(global, local, deltas[k]); // what worker k sends
}
ml::ordered_mean<double>(delta_parts, mean); // the all-reduce, in rank order, divided by the workers
ml::diloco_outer_step<double>(global, mean, momentum, ml::diloco_config{}); // outer lr 0.7, Nesterov 0.9
}

bfloat16 pseudo-gradients. Sending the pseudo-gradient in bfloat16 halves the bytes on the wire (GPT-2 small's 124M parameters: 248 MB instead of 496 MB a round). Three functions fix the arithmetic, so that the transport and the training library agree to the bit:

  • compress_bf16(delta, residual, out): each worker rounds delta + residual to bfloat16 (to nearest, ties to even) and keeps what the rounding dropped as its residual, added to the next round's delta (error feedback: Seide et al. 2014, Karimireddy et al. 2019, 1-bit Adam, Tang et al. 2021). The residual is exact, so over rounds the sum of what a worker sent plus its residual is the sum of its deltas, up to the float additions delta + residual.
  • ordered_sum_bf16(parts, out): the reducing rank decodes each worker's bfloat16 to float (exact) and sums in rank order in float, as ordered_sum.
  • ordered_mean_bf16(parts, out): what every worker holds after the all-reduce and the mean. ts-moveables' allreduce_sum_bf16 leaves bf16(ordered_sum_bf16) on every rank, and the training library divides by the worker count in float.
// Two workers send their pseudo-gradients in bfloat16 (half the bytes); each keeps what the rounding dropped
const std::array<float, 2> delta0{1.0f + 0x1.0p-10f, 0.25f}, delta1{1.0f, -0.75f};
std::array<float, 2> residual0{}, residual1{}; // zero before the first round; each worker's own
std::array<std::uint16_t, 2> wire0{}, wire1{};
ml::compress_bf16(delta0, residual0, wire0); // 1 + 2^-10 goes as 1; residual0[0] = 2^-10, sent next round
ml::compress_bf16(delta1, residual1, wire1);
const std::array<std::span<const std::uint16_t>, 2> wires{wire0, wire1};
std::array<float, 2> bf16_sum{}, bf16_mean{};
ml::ordered_sum_bf16(wires, bf16_sum); // the reducing rank: decoded, summed in rank order in float
ml::ordered_mean_bf16(wires, bf16_mean); // every worker: bf16(sum) / 2, as the bfloat16 all-reduce leaves it

Elastic membership. The worker count may change between rounds, a worker leaving or rejoining. ordered_mean and ordered_mean_bf16 divide by parts.size(), the count of that round's parts, so the outer step needs nothing new. The outer momentum is global state, held identically by every worker, so a rejoining worker receives it with the global weights by broadcast. Its residual restarts at zero: its last round's pseudo-gradient was discarded with the failed round, and a residual from an older checkpoint would re-send a remainder that later rounds already carried; zero loses at most one bfloat16 rounding of one delta. Its inner optimiser state is the training library's choice. Restarting it at zero is the better default: a stale first moment pushes along an old direction, while zero moments, bias-corrected at a late step, begin with short steps.

Why the order of the sum is fixed. Floating-point addition is not associative: (a + b) + c and a + (b + c) can differ in the last bit, and over thousands of steps the differences grow into different models. An all-reduce that adds parts as they arrive, or in the pairing its ring or tree topology happens to give, makes a run depend on timing and on the cluster's shape. ordered_sum defines the one order, parts[0] + parts[1], then + parts[2], and so on, left to right in rank order, in T's own arithmetic; any transport that gathers the parts and sums them in that order gives the same bits, on any number of machines, whatever arrives first. The sum is sequential, not pairwise: pairwise summation is more accurate for many parts, but a data-parallel sum has few parts (the workers) and the order is the contract, reproduced independently by the all-reduce of a library that pins core.

Relationships. ml/optim.hpp (adamw_step, the inner optimiser; squared_norm and clip_coefficient, which merge across shards as these sums do), ml/muon.hpp (the same momentum convention), stats/random.hpp (wyrand::stream, one data stream per worker). The transport, the all-reduce over TCP between machines, belongs to ts-moveables; the device kernels that run the same arithmetic over device memory belong to snicholls-gpu, checked against these functions. Group: snicholls-core.

Implementations. One each: elementwise loops over the caller's spans. Each multiplication and addition of the outer step is its own statement, so no compiler fuses them into an FMA and the bits are the same on every machine (CLAUDE.md, "Machine learning").

Guarantees. constexpr, no allocation, linear in the elements (ordered_sum: parts times elements additions). compress_bf16, ordered_sum_bf16 and ordered_mean_bf16 are noexcept, and are their own specification in float on every machine (core's integer bfloat16 rounding, no FMA possible). noexcept for the standard floating-point types; a number type of the caller's own throws what its arithmetic throws. diloco_config is trivially copyable. out may be parts[0] itself (the sum accumulates into the first part) but no other part; pseudo_gradient's out may be global or local.

Errors. Preconditions, asserted: at least one part; every span the same size. No errors otherwise: a NaN or an infinity in a pseudo-gradient travels on as a NaN or infinity (its residual 0), so the run sees it.

Exactness. With one worker and {.outer_lr = 1, .momentum = 0, .nesterov = false} the outer step computes global - (global - local). That is local to the bit whenever global - local is exact, which Sterbenz's lemma guarantees when local and global are within a factor of two of each other (the usual case: a round moves a weight a little); otherwise it is within one rounding of global - local of local (global = 1, local = 1e-8 in float gives 0, since 1 - 1e-8 rounds to 1). Workers with identical pseudo-gradients d give a mean of d to the bit with one, two or four workers (for four, ((d + d) + d) + d is exactly 4d, checked for every finite float), but not with three ((d + d) + d rounds).

Example.

// Three workers' gradients summed in rank order: the bits every all-reduce must reproduce
const std::array<float, 2> rank0{1e8f, 0.5f}, rank1{1.0f, 0.25f}, rank2{-1e8f, 0.25f};
const std::array<std::span<const float>, 3> parts{rank0, rank1, rank2};
std::array<float, 2> sum{};
ml::ordered_sum<float>(parts, sum); // ((1e8 + 1) - 1e8) = 0: the 1 is lost to rounding in this order

Performance. ordered_sum (four parts) and diloco_outer_step against the same loops by hand (benchmarks/ml/diloco_benchmark.cpp), 1M float elements: level, within the run-to-run noise of 10% either way; about 500 million elements per second for the sum of four parts and 1.1 billion for the outer step, memory bound (Xeon W-3245, Release, Apple clang 17, 2026-11-19, one thread, a loaded machine). The outer step runs once a round, negligible next to the local steps. The bfloat16 pair against the same loops by hand, same machine and date, 1M elements, the mean of three runs: compress_bf16 1.11 billion elements per second (by hand 1.04), ordered_sum_bf16 of four parts 0.90 billion (by hand 0.96): level within the noise, and the sum of bfloat16 parts about twice the float sum's rate, having half the bytes to read.

Tests and demos. tests/ml/diloco_test.cpp, demos/ml/diloco_demo.cpp.

Dependencies. <bit>, <cassert>, <cstddef>, <cstdint>, <span>, <type_traits>, snicholls/core/math/constexpr_math.hpp (for the math_number concept), snicholls/core/ml/minifloat.hpp (bfloat16).

Known issues. The outer momentum is kept in T, as the weights are; the paper keeps it in the weights' precision too. diloco_config holds doubles, converted to T once per step. With bfloat16 pseudo-gradients the sum's own rounding to bfloat16 (once a round, relative 2^-9 of the mean) is not covered by the workers' error feedback: that would need a second residual at the reducing rank, as 1-bit Adam keeps, and is not done. The residual is the worker's own state; it is not averaged.

Status. Experimental, new in 0.15.0; the bfloat16 functions new in 0.16.0.

void snicholls::ml::compress_bf16 ( std::span< const float > delta,
std::span< float > residual,
std::span< std::uint16_t > out )
constexprnoexcept

A pseudo-gradient rounded to bfloat16 for the wire, with error feedback: what the rounding drops is kept and added to the next round's pseudo-gradient, so nothing is lost over rounds.

Per element: v = delta[i] + residual[i] (one float addition); out[i] is the bits of v rounded to the nearest bfloat16, ties to even (to_bfloat16: a NaN stays a quiet NaN with its sign, a value beyond bfloat16's largest finite becomes infinity); residual[i] = v - to_float(out[i]). That subtraction is exact: the bfloat16 is v rounded to 8 significant bits, within a factor of two of v (Sterbenz's lemma; subnormals too, both being multiples of the smallest float subnormal), so v == to_float(out[i]) + residual[i] exactly. A non-finite v, or one that overflows to infinity, carries no residual (0): the NaN or infinity goes on the wire, so the run sees it. Error feedback after Seide et al. (2014, 1-bit SGD), Karimireddy et al. (2019, "Error Feedback Fixes SignSGD"), Tang et al. (2021, 1-bit Adam); low-precision pseudo-gradients in DiLoCo and streaming DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2023, 2025).

Parameters
deltaThis round's pseudo-gradient (pseudo_gradient).
residualThe worker's residual, zero before the first round; updated in place. Must not alias delta.
outThe bfloat16 bits to send.
Precondition
The three spans have one size.

DiLoCo's outer step: SGD with (Nesterov) momentum on the global weights, with the mean pseudo-gradient as the gradient.

For each element, with g the mean pseudo-gradient (already divided by the worker count) and μ the momentum: momentum = μ · momentum + g; step = nesterov ? g + μ · momentum : momentum; global -= outer_lr · step, as PyTorch's SGD(nesterov=True) with no dampening. Each product and sum is its own statement (no FMA), in T.

What a bfloat16 all-reduce followed by the mean gives every worker: the float sum rounded once to bfloat16, decoded, and divided by the worker count.

out[i] = to_float(to_bfloat16(s)) / float(parts.size()), with s the element of ordered_sum_bf16. The contract: ts-moveables' allreduce_sum_bf16 leaves bf16(ordered_sum_bf16) on every rank, and the training library divides by the worker count in float. The sum is rounded (to nearest, ties to even) because the all-gather sends bfloat16; the division is a division, not a multiplication by the reciprocal.
